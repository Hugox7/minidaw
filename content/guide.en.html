01 · Get started in a few minutes

  1. Press Play to hear the demo. Stop playback before editing.
  2. Choose New in the Project bar to start a song. Save your current project first if you want to keep it.
  3. Add Drums, choose a Pattern and Target, then click + Add pattern.
  4. Use Add track for a virtual instrument. Choose its sound, draw notes or place preset chords.
  5. Balance levels, name your song and click Save project.

You can compose without an audio interface. An interface is useful for recording a guitar, bass or microphone.

02 · Find your way around

The Project bar handles naming, opening and saving. The transport controls playback, recording, tempo, bars and master output. The Arrangement shows every track; click a track to edit it below.

The editor's Play button controls the same song as the top button. The elapsed / total duration counter appears in both places. Duration follows the bar count and tempo, excludes the count-in and resets elapsed time when you stop.

  • Arrangement zoom and Audio zoom change the visible scale. Scroll horizontally through longer songs.
  • Link views synchronizes arrangement and editor scrolling.
  • Follow playback follows the playhead. Back to playhead brings it into view.

Tempo is in BPM. Each bar has four beats and sixteen steps: four sixteenth notes per beat.

03 · Draw notes and place chords

Virtual instruments use the piano roll. Changing a sound does not change the notes.

  • Draw / erase: click a cell or hold and drag to paint several notes. Choose New-note duration first.
  • Move: drag a note to change its pitch or position. Drag its right handle to change length.
  • Chords: choose Root, Chord type, Starting octave and Chord duration. Preview the chord, then click where it should start. Click height does not change the chosen pitches.

Copy and clear bars

Choose a Source bar in Copy a bar. Copy to next bar copies to the following bar and advances the source. Copying the last bar adds a bar to the song. Fill song repeats the source across all current bars on this track, including earlier bars, without changing song length.

Occupied destinations require confirmation. Other tracks remain unchanged. Pitches, timing and velocities are copied; a note crossing a source boundary is limited to its portion within the source bar. Empty bars cannot be copied.

Undo copy restores the last operation until another edit. A bar added to song length remains after undo to protect other tracks.

The trash button beside a bar number clears only that bar on the selected track. Later bars do not move. Sustained notes are cut at its boundaries; any portion after the cleared bar starts with a new attack. Undo bar clear restores the last clear until another note edit or track change. Empty bars and playback disable these buttons.

04 · Build a drum track

Choose among 21 rock, pop, funk, disco, electronic, hip-hop, reggae, ska, breakbeat and fill patterns. Descriptions and suggested tempos help you choose; selecting a pattern does not change the song tempo.

  1. Choose a Pattern.
  2. Set Target to one bar or Whole song.
  3. Click + Add pattern, then edit its hits.

Each row is a percussion sound. Click its name to preview it. Click cells to add or remove hits, or hold and drag to paint. New-hit velocity controls hits you draw.

Patterns add to existing hits. Use Clear drums above the grid to start over without deleting the track, kit or mix. Undo clear is available until another hit edit or track change.

Choose Acoustic · Pearl or Synthetic in the Arrangement. Use a bar's trash button to clear just that bar, or Copy a bar to extend a groove.

05 · Connect an instrument and record

Tune your bass or guitar

Stop playback and open Tuner in the header. No audio track is needed. Select Audio input and Channel, enable the input and allow microphone access when prompted. For an instrument on the second interface input, choose Input 2 · right (mono).

Play one string at a time and let it ring. The display shows note, octave, frequency and cents. Negative means flat; positive means sharp. Adjust gently until the green In tune indication appears within ±5 cents. Reference pitch is A = 440 Hz.

The tuner is chromatic. For hard-to-detect bass notes, try the harmonic above the 12th fret; the displayed octave will be one higher. It analyzes the dry input before effects and neither plays sound nor records a take. Close with the cross, Escape or a click outside the dialog. An input enabled only for the tuner is disconnected on close; one already active remains available. Input and channel selection are shared with recording.

Prepare the input

  1. Connect your USB audio interface and plug the instrument or microphone into the appropriate input.
  2. Add and select an Audio track.
  3. Select the interface's Audio input and Channel. Use mono Input 1 or 2 for a single instrument; Stereo 1 + 2 is for a genuine stereo source.
  4. Click Enable input and allow microphone access. This permission also covers instruments.
  5. Play and check Input level. Set interface gain so it does not clip.

A Behringer UMC22 may appear as USB Audio CODEC. Its second input needs Input 2 · right (mono). To identify a device, compare the list before and after unplugging it.

Record a take

Arm the track, choose a zero-, one- or two-bar count-in and click Record with backing. Play after the clicks; they are not recorded. Recording begins at the song's start and continues beyond its last bar, adding bars automatically. Click Stop recording to finish. Existing backing parts play once and do not loop automatically.

The displayed take limit is up to five minutes and 64 MB, rounded down to a whole bar. High sample rates may shorten it. Capture completes and saves automatically at the limit.

Software monitoring is off. Use headphones connected to the interface and its direct monitoring to hear yourself while playing. This is the dry sound. Track volume affects playback, not recorded input level.

Compensate for a consistent delay

Open Take timing offset. Advancing future takes compensates a constant delay. Start at zero and use a short rhythmic test. 100 ms is not a universal setting: it depends on your equipment and configuration.

Compensation preserves take length by finishing capture slightly later. It does not change already recorded takes or calibrate automatically. You can edit it during A/B comparison, including playback; it applies to the next recording. It is locked during capture and saving.

Try again without losing settings

Retake preserves the track and effects. Listen to the old and new versions, then keep one. Accepting the new take replaces all clips on that track. By contrast, Record with backing creates another track when the armed track already has clips.

06 · Edit your takes

In Edit takes, select a clip. Drag its body to move it and its edges to trim or recover hidden audio. The original source stays intact.

  • Snap to beats simplifies musical placement; disable it for fine adjustment.
  • Take position, Start within file and Take duration are numeric fields in seconds. Press Enter or leave the field to confirm.
  • Duplicate take appends a copy after this track's clips, edge to edge on the same lane. Dragging an edge does not create a loop.
  • Restore full audio restores the entire source at the clip position. Undo last edit reverses the last editor operation.

Import audio file appends a file after existing clips. Decodable formats depend on the browser. Files and downloads retrieves the source audio, not the trimmed clip or effected mix. Recordings stay in seconds when tempo changes; they do not time-stretch. Overlapping clips play together.

07 · Balance the mix

Start with track levels, then master volume. Tracks range from −60 to +24 dB in half-decibel steps; master ranges from −60 to +6 dB. Positive gain boosts a quiet take and its existing noise. The speaker button mutes a track.

Watch the meters. If CLIP lights up, reduce the relevant level and click the indicator to reset it. Input clipping must be corrected on the interface. Raising playback gain cannot repair a clipped recording.

The Mixing and effects panel folds by its title. Enable only the treatments you need: pan for left/right placement, EQ for lows/mids/highs, compression to even out levels and reverb for space. Reset EQ returns its three gains to zero. Compression amount adjusts threshold and ratio; advanced controls are optional. A few percent of reverb may be enough.

Select Bass or Electric guitar as Track source to reveal the amp. Enable it and set Character, Gain, Tone and Output level. This shapes playback; it does not choose the interface input. Voice and Other bypass it. Compare effects at similar loudness to avoid preferring a setting only because it is louder.

Guitar and bass pedals

The foldable Pedals section appears before the amp only for audio tracks identified as Bass or Electric guitar. Guitar presets include Crunch, Heavy rock, Metal and Vintage fuzz; bass presets include Light grit, Rock bass and Thick fuzz.

Choose a preset, record some notes and play them back. Adjust Pedal gain, tone and volume. Blend controls the saturated share: 0% is dry; 100% is saturation only. Around 50–65% helps bass retain its low end and attack. Chorus adds width and modulation. Delay adds echoes with a fixed time in milliseconds.

The main Enabled checkbox bypasses the pedalboard without erasing settings; each effect also has its own switch. Presets replace the whole pedalboard, including chorus and delay. Clean disables pedals without changing the amp. Changing track source disables pedals, amp and cabinet while preserving control values.

In the Amp section, Cabinet smooths harsh distortion highs and works even with the amp off. It is lightweight speaker-style filtering, not an impulse response of a real cabinet. Fuzz is inspired by classic heavy pedals rather than an exact model.

The signal path is take → saturation / Blend → chorus → delay → amp → cabinet → mixing. Effects apply during playback and mixed WAV export, never to the stored original. Direct monitoring stays dry. The export effect tail remains four seconds.

08 · Keep songs on your computer

Name the song in the Project bar and click Save project. The portable .minidaw file contains tracks, notes, tempo, mix and used audio sources, including complete originals for trimmed clips. No account is needed.

Open project restores the file and takes. New starts an empty song. Save a copy creates another version; when saving directly to disk, that copy becomes the active file.

A project folder

In supporting browsers such as Chrome or Edge, expand My project folder and choose a folder. Open a project from its list to make later saves update that file. After reloading, authorize the folder again if needed and open its file again. Otherwise a new available filename is created. Refresh reloads the list; Forget this folder deletes no files.

Browsers without direct folder access, including Firefox, use manual opening and downloading. Keep downloaded project files and manage versions in your folders.

Browser autosave is not a disk backup. It keeps the current session in this browser and on this domain. Clearing site data, using another browser or moving from localhost can make it unavailable. Save a .minidaw file to disk.

Latency compensation, device choices and view settings are not included in the project file. You can share .minidaw files with someone using a compatible miniDAW version.

09 · Export a song to listen to

  1. Check tempo, bar count, muted tracks and master volume.
  2. Stop playback and recording; accept or reject any pending retake.
  3. Click Export WAV in the Project bar.
  4. Wait for loading, rendering and download of the .wav named after your project.

The mix is stereo, 16-bit / 44.1 kHz WAV. It includes instruments, drums, takes and effects from the first to last bar, without the count-in. Four seconds are added for releases and effects. Musical duration is limited to five minutes.

A clipping warning means you should lower levels and export again. Export does not automatically normalize or limit the mix. Keep a .minidaw too: WAV export does not save an editable project. Individual clip downloads remain dry source files.

10 · Troubleshooting

The input shows No signal

Check instrument, cable and interface gain. Select the correct input and channel, then enable it. Check microphone permissions and selected devices in both Windows and the browser. For input 2, Windows must expose two channels; a mono device setting may provide only input 1. If the Windows input meter does not move, fix that first.

Meters move but I hear nothing

Check Windows output device, master level, muted tracks and headphones. To hear yourself while playing, use interface direct monitoring. Enabling input does not automatically send the instrument to speakers.

A take is late or duplicates have gaps

Set an appropriate timing offset before recording again. Existing takes do not change. Versions before V0.16.3 shortened compensated takes; missing endings cannot be recovered. Trim any silence in a loop's source before duplicating it.

A pattern mixes with the previous one

Adding patterns preserves existing hits. Clear drums to start over or remove only unwanted hits.

An edit button is disabled

Stop playback or recording. Some operations also require saving to finish or a retake decision. Recording needs an active input and an armed audio track.

Saving or opening fails

Read the error, check free space and folder permissions, and try Save a copy or Download a copy. A failed opening validation preserves the current project. Keep your original file. If a take cannot be saved, retry or download it before closing the tab; it may exist only in memory.

11 · Languages and current limits

Choose Français, English or Español in the header. Switching language changes the interface and URL without reloading or interrupting your song. User-entered track and project names stay unchanged. The language is remembered on this device; an explicit language URL takes priority. All languages on this domain share the same browser project storage. Guide language links open the matching static guide.

Recording requires a compatible browser and localhost or HTTPS. Each take starts from the beginning and continues to manual stop, up to five minutes and 64 MB. The effective whole-bar limit is shown after input activation. Project bundles are limited to 256 MB, 64 MB per audio source and 256 sources.

.minidaw is for editing later; WAV is for listening and sharing. MP3 export and separate stems are not currently provided. The Practice menu opens Bass or Guitar in a separate tab. Native file-picker and browser permission dialogs use your browser or system language.
